# Vision

## Atlas

Remember places that matter.

---

# Why Atlas exists

Every person collects places throughout life.

A beautiful café found during a holiday.

A quiet beach.

A hidden viewpoint.

The perfect place to watch the sunset.

The best bakery in a small French village.

A museum recommended by a friend.

A parking spot that makes visiting a city easy.

A campsite discovered years ago.

These places become part of our personal story.

Unfortunately they become scattered across many different systems.

Apple Maps favourites.

Google Maps lists.

Browser bookmarks.

Screenshots.

Photos.

Notes.

Messages.

Emails.

Memory.

Atlas exists to bring all meaningful places together in one personal library.

---

# Atlas is not about navigation.

Navigation is already solved.

Apple Maps solves navigation.

Google Maps solves navigation.

Atlas solves remembering.

---

# Our philosophy

Places have meaning.

Coordinates do not.

Latitude and longitude are only technical identifiers.

The real value is why a place matters.

Every saved place should answer questions like:

Why did I save this?

Who recommended it?

When did I discover it?

Would I visit again?

What makes it special?

---

# Our goal

Help people build their own atlas of meaningful places.

Not an endless collection of pins.

A curated collection of memories.

---

# Product Principles

## Personal first

Atlas belongs to one person.

Sharing is optional.

The primary experience is private.

---

## Fast capture

Saving a place should take less than ten seconds.

Anything slower discourages collecting.

---

## Beautiful organization

The collection should become more valuable over time.

Users must be able to find places years later.

---

## Minimal friction

Every interaction should feel effortless.

No unnecessary dialogs.

No unnecessary settings.

No unnecessary confirmations.

---

## Offline first

The library should always be available.

Travel often happens without reliable internet.

---

## Apple native

Atlas should feel like an Apple application.

Respect platform conventions.

Do not reinvent standard controls.

---

## Calm software

Atlas should create focus.

No notifications.

No feeds.

No infinite scrolling.

No engagement mechanics.

No dopamine loops.

The application serves the user.

The user never serves the application.

---

# Success

The measure of success is not:

Number of saved places.

Number of daily users.

Time spent in the application.

Instead success means:

"I always know where I saved that place."

"I trust Atlas."

"I enjoy adding new places."

"I can find a place years later."

"My library becomes more valuable every year."
